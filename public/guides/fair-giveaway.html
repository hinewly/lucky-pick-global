How to Run a Fair Giveaway: 5 Rules Every Host Should Follow

Nothing damages trust faster than a giveaway that looks rigged. Whether you're giving away a product to your newsletter subscribers or picking a winner at a family game night, the same principle applies: decide the rules before you see the entries, and let randomness do the picking.

Rule 1: Write the entry rules down first

Before you collect a single entry, decide and publish: who can enter, what counts as an entry, when the deadline is, and how the winner will be chosen. Changing rules mid-way — even innocently — is the most common source of "this giveaway was unfair" complaints. If entrants know the selection method in advance, nobody can accuse you of choosing favorites afterwards.

Rule 2: Use a random draw, not your memory

Scrolling a comment list and "pointing" at a winner feels random but isn't: you're biased by order, names, and avatars without realizing it. Use a tool that generates a truly random pick from your full entry list. If you need multiple winners, draw them all at once from the same pool — and note who was drawn so you don't redraw the same person.

Rule 3: Keep the draw reproducible

A good practice for public giveaways: save or screenshot the entry list before the draw, and record the moment of the draw and the result. If anyone questions the outcome, you can show exactly what happened. For game nights at home, this is overkill — but for anything with an audience, a ten-second record saves a lot of arguing.

Rule 4: Handle ties and invalid entries in advance

Decide before drawing: what happens if an entry is a duplicate? What if the drawn winner doesn't respond within 48 hours? The standard answer is simple — invalid entries are removed before the draw, and a non-responsive winner gets one redraw among the remaining entries. Write this down in your rules from Rule 1 and you'll never have to improvise.

Rule 5: Announce honestly, including the odds

When announcing the winner, mention how many entries there were and how the winner was picked ("drawn at random from 237 entries"). This costs you one sentence and buys you lasting credibility. And if your giveaway involves prizes of real value, check the promotion and sweepstake rules that apply in your country or state — some jurisdictions have specific requirements for public prize draws.

A quick checklist

  • ☐ Rules published before entries open
  • ☐ Entry list saved before the draw
  • ☐ Winner drawn with a random picker
  • ☐ Tie / no-response rule decided in advance
  • ☐ Announcement includes entry count and draw method
